Friction — AI Study Tool from Your Notes

2026 · Lead developer

Friction turns your own lecture notes into interactive lessons. Paste notes or upload a PDF and it builds a slideshow, quiz, flashcards and fill-in-the-blank exercises from the actual content — not generic explanations — with some slides carrying an interactive simulation seeded from your own numbers. PDFs are parsed in the browser, falling back to OCR for scans and photos. Named for "desirable difficulty" in learning science: material you work at is material you retain. A signed-out demo runs a full lesson, no account needed.

Witness — AI Pre-Release Tester for Web Apps

2026 · Solo

Give Witness a running web app's URL and an AI agent drives a real browser through its core journeys, reporting each bug with evidence, steps to reproduce and a Playwright test that fails until the bug is fixed. Its rule is "AI proposes, code proves": every finding is replayed in a fresh browser, and code, not the AI, decides how confident to be. On a benchmark app with 23 seeded bugs, 14–21% of the AI's raw claims were false; none survived verification, and clean runs raised zero false alarms.

TypeScript Playwright Express React 19 Claude Zod Vercel AI SDK OpenRouter

Closure — AI Feedback for Rejected Applicants

2026 · Solo

One of five winners at ZEIL's AI Hackathon 2026, built solo in a day; it earned me a paid AI internship at ZEIL. Closure drafts honest feedback and a next step for every applicant a recruiter rejects. A writer model drafts, code verifies every CV quote, and a critic model checks the message and the recruiter's note against the NZ Human Rights Act, holding discriminatory reasons instead of rewording them. RippleGuard — Credit-Risk Stress Simulator

2026 · Team of 3

Winner of Challenge 3 at the Innovate AI Hackathon 2026, run with BNZ and AWS. gitme-ai — AI Git Commit Generator

2026 · Solo

A command-line tool, published on PyPI, that reads your staged git diff and writes a Conventional Commits message for it. It's local-first: by default it runs Gemma on your own machine through Ollama, so your diffs never leave it, and you can switch to OpenAI or OpenRouter in its config. You can add context the diff can't show, such as "a hotfix for the login bug", and copy the message straight to your clipboard.
